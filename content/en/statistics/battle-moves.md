# Battle moves

**How do you change a creature's battle moves?** With gene edits. A creature holds five moves, one in each slot. Once its three stats all reach level {{statCap}}, each gene edit can mutate one ability: the slot rolls again from its own list, and the roll never gives back the move it held.

## Gene edits

Battles in the Holo-Arena fill a creature's Mutation Progress, and each full bar is one gene edit. A creature first spends its edits on its stats, Combat, Agility, and Health, one level per edit, up to level {{statCap}} in each. That makes {{fullPotential}} edits, and the game calls the result maximum genetic potential. Until then Mutate Ability stays shut, with the line "Cannot edit until creature has reached max potential".

Edits keep coming after that. The bar grows longer with each edit a creature has earned, from {{firstEdit}} progress for the first to {{lastEdit}} from edit {{lastFrom}} on, and it stays there. An edit earned early waits unspent, so a creature can come to its full potential with edits in hand for its moves.

What a level of a stat adds is on [creature stats](/guides/creature-stats).

## Retroviral Pellets

![Retroviral Pellet](/creatures/retroviral-pellet.webp)

A Retroviral Pellet is one gene edit in a pill. Feed it to a companion and it adds one unspent edit, the same as a full Mutation Progress bar, so after full potential it pays for one mutation of a move. It does not pick a move by itself: the mutation rolls as any other.

A won battle can drop one, most often the daily challenge of Iteration: Oceanus, {{dailyPellet}} of the time, and a system champion, {{championPellet}}. The Arena League section below lists every battle.

## The Arena League

Pet battles are fought at Holo-Arenas, and the Arena League runs them. A player meets it in this order.

1. **Iteration: Oceanus.** A collector at the Holo-Arena aboard the Space Anomaly. They teach the first battle and hand over a Retroviral Pellet, then a Holo-Arena Invitation.
2. **Challengers.** A Challenger's Invitation marks a Holo-Arena on a nearby planet. Beat the challenger and the next invitation comes on its own.
3. **The champion.** After every {{challengers}} challenger wins, the next invitation is a Champion's Invitation instead: the system champion, who fights with creatures from the planets of that system. Beat them and a new Challenger's Invitation starts the round again, so it is {{challengers}} challengers, then a champion, for as long as you play.
4. **Oceanus again.** On request Oceanus gives a training battle, which pays little but always hands out a Challenger's Invitation. Each day they also hold a hard daily challenge with the same team for every Traveller; it pays the most Nanites and the best pellet chance.

The other tables of the list need no invitation. A challenger gives a pellet {{challengerPellet}} of the time, a system or outpost table {{stationPellet}}. Which building holds which table is not researched yet.

### Medals and their eggs

Wins count toward five medals in the Catalogue, each with ten ranks and a title per rank. At the top rank of each, Oceanus's Arena League Rewards give one egg for free. Each hatches with all three grades at S and one of the fixed kits below. {{dailyWins}} daily challenges won bring the Wandering Shrimp Tank, {{championWins}} champions beaten the Aeron Crab, and {{battleWins}} battles won the Geno-Prawn.

## Eggs of your companions

A companion can lay an egg, a Companion Egg, and the egg carries its parent's battle grades. What the game asks of the parent:

- **Fed and petted.** Hunger and loneliness both at {{eggMood}} or less; the card names what is missing ("Feeling hungry", "Feeling sad", "Requires additional nutrition"). Trust is not among the reasons the card gives.
- **In its native climate**, and grown at least {{growDays}} days if it hatched from an egg itself.
- **One egg at a time.** {{eggHours}} hours between two eggs, no other egg of yours lying nearby, and a free egg slot.

The egg hatches {{hatchHours}} hours later. Before that the Egg Sequencer on the Space Anomaly can re-sequence it with ingredients: size, temperament, colour, and accessories can shift, and the body can change, but the battle grades and moves stay out of its reach.

The battle grades come from the species, and the egg copies them, so a hatchling has its parent's letters: an all-S creature lays all-S eggs, and a medal egg's creature, the Wandering Shrimp Tank among them, keeps its three S in its eggs. Its five moves are drawn again on hatching, from the same species roll, so a hatchling starts with the moves its parent first had, not the ones mutated since. Whether a hatchling keeps its parent's gene edits is not researched yet.

Every egg you hatch counts toward the Hatch Eggs medal: {{hatchMedal}} hatched bring the Corrupted Quadruped.

## The five slots

Every creature draws from the same lists, whatever its affinity; only its moves of its own affinity take that affinity. There are {{moves}} moves across the five slots, and each slot has a job of its own.

A new creature draws each slot by the weights of its list: that is Chance. A mutation draws again from the same list but skips the move the slot holds, so a heavy move held makes the others likelier, and On a mutation shows the least and the most a move can get. To land one move, keep mutating that slot; a light move takes many tries.

### Slot 1: plain hits

The hit of the creature's own affinity comes up {{ownHit}} of the time, and it is the one to keep. A hit of another affinity is worth it only against the creatures it is strong against, and the physical hit is never strong against anything.

<!-- widget:moves1 -->

### Slot 2: heavier attacks

The two wild moves are near useless: physical, with an extra half chance to miss. Barrage lands three hits in one turn; hit-and-wound and the multi-turn hit spread their damage over later turns, which a fight can end before.

<!-- widget:moves2 -->

### Slot 3: attacks with a catch

Every move here weighs the same, so each has the same chance. Avoid the reckless hit that heals the enemy, and mind the two that hurt your own creature. Players count buff removal, which Purge does, among the strongest picks.

<!-- widget:moves3 -->

### Slot 4: heals and shields

The plain Heal lands on the turn you use it; heal over time and the delayed heal pay later, and Absorb heals only while the enemy hits. How much each heals in the end is not on this page yet.

<!-- widget:moves4 -->

### Slot 5: tricks

Players rate Stun, which costs the enemy a turn, and Change affinity, which turns the enemy into the affinity your creature hits hardest, above the rest. Revive is no revive: it acts as the sacrifice for defence.

<!-- widget:moves5 -->

## Names

The game names a move by its kind and the creature's affinity, so one move shows a different name on a Fire creature than on a Frost one. A kind can have up to three names; which of them a creature shows is not researched yet.

<!-- widget:names -->

## Fixed kits

The five medal eggs always hold the same five moves, one in each slot, with no list to draw from. What a mutation does to them is not researched yet.
