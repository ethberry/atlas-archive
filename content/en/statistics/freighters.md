# Freighters

**How do you get a class S capital freighter?** Save one from a pirate attack in a pirate system, before you own a freighter. Every try rolls the class again, and a pirate system gives class S {{pirateS}} of the time, the most of any system.

## Regular and capital

The game has two kinds of freighter. A regular freighter is the smaller ship you see in the fleets of a system, and you buy it. A capital freighter is the giant one, and you get it only by saving it from a battle. Besides where you get it and how it looks, the kind sets one thing: the slots it starts with.

<!-- widget:kinds -->

Everything else follows the class, and a freighter keeps its class for good: no terminal, item, or reward upgrades it. What grows is the inventory. A Cargo Bulkhead opens one slot, general or technology, up to the most the class allows, and that most is the same for both kinds.

<!-- widget:limits -->

So a small regular class C freighter and a giant capital class C both stop at {{generalC}} slots and {{techC}} for technology, and only a class S of either kind reaches {{generalS}} and {{techS}}. The hyperdrive and fleet bonus come from the class too, and every freighter holds 6 ships in its hangar. If you want the most a freighter can do, you want class S; if you want the giant, you want a capital one.

## The battle on arrival

The game counts your play time and your warps since the last battle. Once {{hours}} hours and {{warps}} warps have passed, a battle is due, and the next warp brings it: pirates attack a freighter as you arrive. It never comes in an uncharted system or an anomaly system, such as one with an Atlas Interface or a black hole, nor with space combat turned off. Save the freighter and talk to its captain. While you own no freighter, the captain gives you that one for free. Or take the payment instead: a Cargo Bulkhead, nanites, and standing.

Who attacks depends on you and on the system. Without a freighter of your own the pirates send fighters. Once you own one, they can send a dreadnought, whose kill pays one of the [freighter modules](/guides/freighter-modules), and in a pirate system they always do. Which battle a system brings is drawn from the system itself, so it is the same every time it is due there. Some systems also bring a dreadnought on every arrival, battle due or not, and [dreadnoughts](/guides/dreadnoughts) has them.

<!-- widget:battles -->

Your first two battles save a regular freighter. From the third on, the freighter you save is a capital one. A battle counts the moment it starts: win it and turn the freighter down, and it is still one of your two, and so is a dreadnought battle. Owning a freighter in a pirate system skips that rule: even the first battle there is a dreadnought.

## One ship, many classes

A system holds one capital freighter: every battle there saves the same ship, the same shape and the same size. Only its class is rolled again on every try, from a number the game hands the ship as it appears, not from the system. No one can tell the class before the battle. So find a shape you like with the [capital freighters page](/capital-freighter-finder), save the game in a neighbouring system with the battle due, warp in, and load that save again until the ship comes at S.

## Wealth and class

<!-- widget:classes -->

In a pirate system a try comes at S {{pirateS}} of the time and at C {{pirateC}}. Every try is a new roll: twenty misses in a row do not make the next try any likelier. So luck spreads wide. Half the players get class S within {{pirateHalf}} tries, nine in ten within {{pirateNine}}, and an unlucky one in a hundred needs more than {{pirateAll}}. On average it takes {{pirateMean}}.

<!-- widget:tries -->

A high wealth system gives S only {{highS}} of the time, so half the players need {{highHalf}} tries, and a low wealth system never gives S. So fly to a pirate system first: about {{pirateOneIn}} systems is one, marked with a skull on the galaxy map.

## Can you buy a class S?

Yes, a regular one. A system has three regular freighters of its own, and its fleets fly those three. The [regular freighters](/freighter-finder) page shows every shape a regular freighter can take. Each freighter's class is rolled from the [wealth](/guides/economy) of the system every time a fleet arrives: class S is {{pirateS}} of them in a pirate system, {{highS}} at high wealth, {{mediumS}} at medium, and never at low. So a fleet is not the same on every visit, and coming back to one system shows new classes.

The price grows with the slots and the class. The game starts at {{costBase}} million units for {{costSlots}} slots, multiplies by the slots over {{costSlots}} to the power {{costPower}}, and adds {{bonusB}} for class B, {{bonusA}} for A, and {{bonusS}} for S. A small class S costs {{smallS}} million units, a large one {{largeS}} million.

<!-- widget:prices -->

A capital freighter is never for sale in a fleet. It comes only from a battle, for free while you own no freighter, and you can try again from a save. So unless you want the look of a regular one, farm the battle.

## Supercharged slots

A freighter's supercharged slots lie in its technology inventory. The class sets how many: one at C, up to four at S.

<!-- widget:supercharged -->

They all lie in the top three rows of the technology inventory, and where they lie belongs to the ship, not to the try. Every try at a system's capital freighter puts them in the same places: class C shows the first of four places, B the first two, A the first three, and S all four. A regular freighter keeps its places the same way, each of the three of a system its own.

The places never move. A Cargo Bulkhead opens a slot but does not move a supercharged one, and since the class never changes, neither does the count. A supercharged slot can lie in a slot that is still closed; it works once a Cargo Bulkhead opens it.

## Upgrade modules

No merchant sells a freighter upgrade module. A destroyed pirate dreadnought gives one at class S, and a derelict freighter one of any class. What each module raises, and every place that gives one, is on [freighter technology](/guides/freighter-technology); how to farm the class S ones from dreadnoughts is on [freighter modules](/guides/freighter-modules).

Once you own a freighter, a pirate system brings another battle: [dreadnoughts](/guides/dreadnoughts).
