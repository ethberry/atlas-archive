# Economy

**Which system pays best?** One of high wealth. A system's economy has a type and a wealth, low, medium, or high, shown as one, two, or three stars on the galaxy map. Wealth decides what a system pays, and the type changes nothing you could plan on. Pirate systems, marked Pirate Controlled on the galaxy map, hold more class S tools, but weak ones.

## Wealth

A system offers about the same number of tools at any wealth, and a pirate one a little fewer. What wealth changes is what those tools are: class S is {{lowS}} of tools at low wealth, {{highS}} at high, and {{pirateS}} in a pirate system, and {{lowTouch}} of tools hold adjacent slots at low wealth against {{highTouch}} at high. Pirate systems sit apart, with only {{pirateTouch}}. [Multitool classes](/guides/multitool-classes) shows why. Wealth does not move the [technology merchants](/guides/technology-merchants) a station sells.

Wrecks barely notice: a medium wealth system holds {{mediumShips}}, a pirate one {{pirateShips}}.

The median stays between {{economyLow}} and {{economyHigh}} tools in a system, whatever the type.

Classes do not follow the type either. Only pirate and abandoned systems move them, and an uncharted one pays no tool at all. Uncharted means a system with no lifeform, no space station, and no shop or monolith.

## Which system will be pirate

Stars in a voxel are not shuffled: a system's number sets the odds of its station. Low numbers are almost all inhabited, the middle holds the pirates, and high numbers are where abandoned stations gather. So coordinates tell you what to expect before you jump, and distance plays a part too, as [where to look](/guides/where-to-look) shows.
