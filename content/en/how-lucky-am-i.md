# How lucky am I?

**How lucky is the multitool or ship you found?** Enter it here and you get a score from 0 to 100, where 100 is the luckiest. The score compares your item with others of the same type and class.

<!-- widget:calculator -->

## How to read your item

Take every module off first, because a module adds to an item's numbers and they stop saying anything about luck. Each field runs only as far as the type and class can reach.

For a multitool or a [staff](/guides/staffs), enter Damage Potential and Scanner Range as its card shows them, in your inventory or your storage. Mining shows on no card, so it is not asked, and a pistol has no base damage, so it asks for Scanner Range alone. The page turns the card into the base stats of [multitool stats](/guides/multitool-stats) itself.

For a ship, enter only one you have claimed, and fly it while you read the card, because the numbers move with the ship you are flying. All four are the numbers on the card, the same as [ship stats](/guides/ship-stats). A working starter cannon adds 20 to 41 damage, so a ship that has one reads a little luckier on damage than it is.

A ship upgraded at a station is scored as if it had been found at that class. What an upgrade does to a ship's stats is not researched yet, as [how to upgrade a ship](/guides/ship-upgrades) says, so the score of an upgraded ship's stats may not mean luck.

## How the score works

Each stat is placed among items of that type and class, and so are the supercharged slots: scattered slots are the usual case, a [tetris figure](/guides/figures) of four adjacent slots is rare, and [the square](/guides/square) is rarer still. On a ship, [ship figures](/guides/ship-figures) shows the same. Slots count by what they are worth to a weapon and its modules, as [supercharged slots](/guides/supercharged-slots) shows: a T is worth a little less than the other figures, and the square the most, so a T scores below a line or a corner. The score then asks how often all of those come together this well, so one great stat cannot hide two poor ones. Stats are never added up, because each has limits of its own.

An upgrade is not luck. A multitool's class and its damage come from one roll, so damage outside what the class allows gives away a multitool that was upgraded rather than found at that class. Every staff upgraded at a station to one class holds the same numbers, so such a staff gets nothing for them and only its slots count. [How to upgrade a multitool](/guides/multitool-upgrades) has the rules, and [multitool classes](/guides/multitool-classes) shows where class S multitools come from without an upgrade. The [multitools](/tool) and [crashed ships](/ship) in the catalog show what a lucky find looks like.
